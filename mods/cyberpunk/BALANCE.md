# Blackout Protocol — faction matchups

Blackout Protocol fights through precision, withdrawal and a protected support network. Steel Tide's conventional armor can punish a Nexus army that stands still and trades until its shields are gone. Both factions use the same map, starting resources, population, territory and victory rules.

## Strengths have a price

| Nexus tool | What it gains | What it gives up / how to answer it |
|---|---|---|
| Sentinel | Moving fire, speed, extra reach and a recovering shield | A thin medium-armored hull loses sustained cannon duels. Flanks, corners and pursuit that denies recovery reduce its advantage. |
| Aegis + Photon Lancer | A shielded screen in front of precise, uninterceptable anti-armor beams | Aegis pays for protection instead of damage. Lancers are vulnerable to light raiders and aircraft; a mixed screen costs more than buying guns alone. |
| Conductor + Suture | Shorter reloads and field repairs for eligible nearby machines | Small support radii constrain formations. Suture repair needs the recipient to stop firing; identical support pulses do not stack. |
| Arc Strider | Electrical area damage against clustered light forces | High investment, a slow chassis and no answer to aircraft. Spread out and attack its screen. |
| Breach Crawler / Breach Spire | Long-range area bombardment | Deployment or immobility, minimum range, inaccurate fire and friendly blast damage. |
| Longbow / Void Battery | Long-range missile pressure on entrenched positions | Thin mobile hull or expensive fixed infrastructure; minimum range, visible missiles, point-defense interception and friendly splash. |
| Archon | A long-range siege beam that bypasses missile defense | A slow, expensive walker with a blind inner range and no anti-air weapon. It needs reconnaissance and escorts. |
| Warden / Halo | Early dedicated air defense | No ground attack. Ground pressure forces the owner to split its investment. |
| Palisade / Aurora / Aegir | Longer-range air cover and nuclear interception without veteran rank | Power and screening costs. Missile-based versions can be intercepted; a last-second cluster does not guarantee nuclear protection. |
| Firewall | A moving point-defense magazine | No offensive weapon. Sustained salvos can exhaust the magazine; beams and ordinary shells bypass it. |
| Null Chapel | Blocks hostile radar and remote cameras | Requires full power and reveals the transmitter's location. Visual scouts and sonar remain effective. |
| Locust | A fast, expendable breaching strike | A fragile airframe, an explicit target order, ten-unit limit and no return trip. Prepared air defense can stop it before impact. |
| Tempest | A concentrated bombing pass against ground forces and buildings | Two runs before returning to its own Skyport, no air-to-air weapon, and a vulnerable approach/rearming cycle. |
| Cutlass | Long-range anti-ship missiles | Cannot attack ground, air or submerged targets. Interception blunts its salvos. |
| Leviathan / Ferryman | Surface precision fire / a rebuilding autonomous strike wing | Slow, expensive hulls. Submarines, air attacks and escorts that shoot down drones create different counterplay. |
| Ravager | Armored movement across water without a transport | Its target domain changes afloat, exposing it to torpedoes; it does not replace a specialist ship. |
| Fabricator / Stitch | Amphibious construction / transportable ground construction | Fabricators cannot board transports. Stitches cannot build at sea; normal HQ territory still limits distant construction. |

## Economy and timing

A Nexus is a real headquarters and an expensive expansion. It grants territory and production slots, trains construction units and defends its immediate area. Combat recruitment requires production facilities.

Taps and Brine Taps each have three extraction levels. Flux Reactors have three power levels. Upgrading a protected deposit grows the economy without another expansion, but concentrates an expensive asset in one location. High-tier power also concentrates risk: a destroyed reactor damages nearby friends. Leave room around the grid and keep reserve power for production, repairs, jamming and weapons. Unit shields recharge after their damage delay; they are not a substitute for a working economy.

The Foundry opens the ground line. The Skyport follows it and immediately offers reconnaissance and expendable strike drones. The Signal Spire unlocks advanced specialists across the production lines, and the Oracle unlocks the heavy investment. Losing prerequisites interrupts this network. Permanent upgrades to vanilla doctrines are not part of Blackout's roster; its strength comes from its own equipment and support rules.

## Fighting Steel Tide

Start on an ordinary map with equal settings. The eight Veyra operation maps include authored starting forces and should not be used to measure an equal-start duel.

Use Needles against light pressure and Sentinel withdrawals to buy room. Build Wardens when aircraft appear. Against conventional armor, add Lancers behind Aegis shields rather than simply increasing Sentinel numbers. Cannons and tank destroyers punish a stationary medium-armored line. A Reboot Bay and shield rotation can turn a surviving army into an economic advantage.

Use reconnaissance before committing siege. Firewalls protect a moving column from missiles, while Lancers and Archons attack through enemy interception. Against dispersed targets, precision matters; against a mass of light vehicles, Striders and artillery earn their investment. Keep the firing lane clear of your own assault troops.

At sea, match escorts to the threat. Skiffs and Undertows provide sonar, Aegirs protect the air corridor, Cutlasses attack surface hulls from range, and Leviathans finish a surface engagement. A carrier-only fleet loses badly to prepared air defense. Offshore income needs protection, and a landing barge carrying a Stitch opens a different route from a direct fleet battle.

## Current balance evidence

Version 1.2.0 was checked with unchanged game rules and vanilla definitions. Controlled combat probes cover armor, light swarms, aircraft, submarines, interception and mixed fleets in both deployment directions. A Sentinel loses a stationary tank duel but wins the same engagement when ordinary move orders preserve its standoff range. Mixed armies win or lose according to their composition; the roster was not tuned to make every corresponding unit tie.

Thirty fresh-start computer matches used Standard difficulty, fog, 1,500 starting metal, a 150 population cap, three seeds and both seat assignments. Across the three land maps, Nexus won four, Steel Tide won five, and nine remained unresolved at 30 minutes. Across two naval maps, Nexus won none, Steel Tide won six, and six remained unresolved. Every match kept faction ownership clean: no vanilla unit or building entered the Nexus roster. Unresolved games are not counted as wins or proof of equality.

These observations establish functional matchups, not competitive certification. The stock computer handles its vanilla economy and counters more effectively than Nexus's movement, shield rotation and specialized naval logistics. Naval AI performance is an open balance issue; human PvP parity and the operation atlas's full completion time have not been measured. The installed faction is playable against Steel Tide, with these limits stated rather than hidden by matching its stats.
